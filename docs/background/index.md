# Why sinks?

Hospital sinks and drains are a recognised reservoir for Gram-negative
opportunists such as *Pseudomonas*, *Klebsiella*, *Enterobacter*,
*Citrobacter*, *Serratia* and *Stenotrophomonas*. Biofilms in the drain trap can
grow up towards the strainer and be splashed onto hands, equipment and
patients. When the same species turns up in a sink and in a patient, two
questions follow:

1. **Are they the same strain?** Whole-genome sequencing answers this: two
   isolates only a few allele differences apart across the core genome are the
   same strain.
2. **Which way did it move?** Genomics cannot answer this. The direction of
   spread, and whether the sink was the source, has to come from the
   epidemiology: sampling dates, room layout and whether the patient screened
   negative on admission.

sinklink answers the first question as carefully as the data allow, and puts
the dates next to each pair so the second can be judged.
